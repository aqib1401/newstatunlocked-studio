What if you aged ten times slower? Not a little. Ten years for every one. It starts on your fifteenth birthday. Ten years pass. You're twenty-five on paper, but you look sixteen, so the cinema still won't sell you a ticket to a scary movie. Your little brother Sam is suddenly taller than you, and loves calling you kiddo. Sounds amazing, right? Except you're the only one who slowed down. Fifteen years in, Biscuit, your birthday puppy, is old and grey. You've barely aged a year and a half. Because every animal runs on its own clock. A mouse is old at two. A Greenland shark may live four hundred years. Your clock is even slower. You could reach eight hundred. So you use the time. You study medicine for forty years straight. Now you're the only surgeon who can fix a newborn baby's heart, because nobody else lives long enough to learn it. Hundreds of tiny hearts, still beating because of you. Sixty years in, Sam is an old man with a few months left, and he hands you a cure. Take one pill, and you grow old with him, but the babies lose you. And it only works before midnight. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. Sam squeezes your hand. Don't stay young alone, kiddo. Seven. Six. Stay, and you'll save babies for six hundred more years. Five. Four. Would you stay young, or grow old with Sam?
