SCRIPT:
What if you could teleport anywhere? One blink, and you're on the other side of the world... and that's exactly where it goes wrong. You test it with a soccer ball first. You send it from your backyard to a park on the far side of the planet, and it doesn't just appear. It blasts through a fence at more than two thousand kilometers an hour. Why? Because Earth is a giant spinning merry go round. The ground under you is already racing east at over a thousand kilometers an hour, and so is the ground on the far side, but over there, east points the opposite way. Jump across, and you keep your old speed... so you arrive moving backwards, twice as fast as a passenger jet. So you learn to cheat. You stop dead first, then pop out gently, like stepping off an escalator. And now nothing is out of reach. Breakfast in Paris. Lunch on a beach in Brazil. A sunset on the top of Mount Everest, where every breath holds about a third of the oxygen you're used to, and the wind is cold enough to freeze your fingers in minutes. So you go bigger... the Moon. Spacesuit first, because out there without one, you'd have about fifteen seconds before you pass out. You land in total silence. You jump, and you float six times higher than on Earth. And Earth hangs in a black sky, small enough to hide behind your thumb. But this is where it gets strange. Light takes time to travel, and you don't. So you jump to Mars, where light from Earth can take up to twenty minutes to arrive. Point an impossibly powerful telescope home, and you're watching Earth as it was twenty minutes ago. Your backyard. And you... still standing in it, about to leave. You beat your own light here. So you keep going. To a star a hundred light years away, where the light reaching you left Earth a hundred years ago. Through that impossible telescope, it's old cars, steam trains, and your great great grandparents, walking down the street. Every star in the sky is a window into a different year of Earth's history. So if you could stand by any star and watch any moment from the past... which moment would you teleport to see?

FACTS USED:
1. Earth's surface moves east at up to about 1,670 km/h at the equator (over 1,000 km/h at most inhabited latitudes); at the antipode, the same eastward motion points the opposite way, so the relative speed is about double: high
2. Air pressure on the summit of Everest is about a third of sea level, so each breath holds about a third of the oxygen: high
3. Exposed to vacuum, a person stays conscious for roughly 15 seconds (NASA): high
4. Moon gravity is about one sixth of Earth's, so a jump goes about six times higher: high
5. Light from Earth takes about 3 to 22 minutes to reach Mars, depending on where the planets are: high
6. Light reaching a star 100 light years away left Earth 100 years ago (the telescope is the fiction): high
7. Earth seen from the Moon is about 2 degrees across, about the width of a thumb at arm's length: medium
