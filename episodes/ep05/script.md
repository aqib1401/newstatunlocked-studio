SCRIPT:
What if your heart beat only six times a minute? Not sixty. Six. Your doctor checks your pulse, then checks again. But underwater, you're a legend. You hold your breath for five minutes, while your friend Leo taps out at forty seconds. Diving seals and whales slow their hearts down to save oxygen. A blue whale's heart can drop to two beats a minute. But yours never speeds back up. So when you stand up too fast, the room goes black. Run for the bus, and your knees fold, because your muscles beg for blood and your brain gets none. Then, at the lake, your little brother Sam slips under, past the rope. The lifeguard is a minute away. Sam has seconds. Five. Four. Three. Swim hard, and you might black out underwater too. Wait, and he might never come up. With a heart this slow, would you dive in?

FACTS USED:
1. A normal resting heart rate is about 60-100 beats a minute: high
2. Diving mammals (seals, whales) slow their heart rate when they dive to conserve oxygen (the dive reflex): high
3. A blue whale's heart was measured dropping to about 2 beats a minute during dives (Goldbogen et al., 2019): high
4. Standing up quickly can briefly reduce blood flow to the brain and cause dizziness or blackout; exercise normally needs a faster heart to supply muscles: high
