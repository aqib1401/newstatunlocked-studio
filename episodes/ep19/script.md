SCRIPT:
What if you never felt hungry again? Not even a little. You skip lunch to finish your robot, and your team wins the science fair. You skip dinner and beat the game in one night. But then your hands start shaking, and the room tilts. Because hunger isn't a weakness. It's an alarm. A hormone called ghrelin, made mostly in your stomach, tells your brain it's time to eat. Your brain runs on sugar from food, and when that drops too low, you can pass out. And your alarm is gone. Then a snowstorm traps you and your little brother Sam in a mountain cabin. You haven't eaten in two days, and you never noticed. One small granola bar. Your vision blurs. Five. Four. Three. If you pass out, the fire dies, and you both freeze. But Sam is eight, and he's begging. Eat it, or give it to Sam?
