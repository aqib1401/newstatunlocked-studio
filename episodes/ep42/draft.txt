What if you grew one meter taller... every single day? Day one, you wake up two meters sixty tall, your feet hanging off the end of the bed, and your mom sighs and buys you new shoes. Day two, three meters sixty. You're now taller than the tallest person who ever lived, and you have to crawl through your school's front door. Everyone wants a selfie. The basketball coach cries happy tears. But the thing that's going to stop you isn't a ceiling... it's math. When you get twice as tall, you don't get twice as heavy. You get eight times heavier, because you grow taller, wider and deeper all at once. Your bones only get thicker, so they can't keep up. Day five, six meters sixty. You're taller than a giraffe and as heavy as a rhino, but your bones are only four times thicker than they used to be. Your house can't hold you, so you sleep in the park under a circus tent. Day ten, eleven meters. You weigh nineteen tonnes, more than three elephants, and every step puts seven times more pressure on your bones than they were built for. Your heart is struggling too. A giraffe needs about double your blood pressure just to push blood up its neck, and your brain is now higher than a giraffe's head. Stand up too fast... and you black out. And you're cooking. Your body makes heat everywhere inside it, but it only loses heat through your skin, and your skin is falling behind. So firefighters spray you with hoses, like an elephant at a waterhole. Day twenty. Twenty one meters, a hundred and twenty tonnes, and every step cracks the road. Even lying down, your own weight squeezes your lungs, just like a whale stranded on a beach. So you do the only thing that makes sense... you walk into the sea. The moment the water lifts you, the pain stops. And that's not luck. The biggest animal that has ever lived, the blue whale, lives in the ocean for exactly this reason. Only water can carry that much weight. Day thirty. You're floating next to one, thirty one meters long, finally comfortable. And back on the beach, your mom is waving... holding a pair of shoes the size of a boat.
