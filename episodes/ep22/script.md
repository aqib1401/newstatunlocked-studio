SCRIPT:
What if you could see in the dark? Not a little better. Perfectly. Night one, the power cuts out at a sleepover. Everyone screams, but you calmly walk to the kitchen and bring your best friend Mia a snack. Night two, a blackout hits your whole building, and you lead your neighbors down twelve floors of pitch black stairs. Nobody trips once. But then the daytime starts to hurt. Day three, you squint at the sun with tears running down your face. Day four, a camera flash in class leaves you blind for an hour. Here's why. Cats can see in much less light than we can. They have a mirror at the back of their eyes. It bounces the light back for a second look, and that's why their eyes glow in photos. Now your eyes have that mirror too, so every bright light feels twice as bright. Night five, your little brother Sam wanders into the woods behind your house. The searchers' torches miss him, but you spot him, stumbling toward the river. Then a rescue helicopter roars in, and its searchlight sweeps toward you. Your sunglasses are back at home. Mia, searching beside you, grabs your arm. That light could blind you for good! Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. Sam slips on the muddy bank. Seven. Six. The light sweeps closer. Five. Four. Shut your eyes and lose him in the dark, or keep looking and maybe never see again. Would you keep your eyes open?
