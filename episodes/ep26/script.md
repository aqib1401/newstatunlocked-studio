SCRIPT:
What if your voice was a hundred times louder? Not loud. Jet engine loud. You sneeze in the library, and every book jumps off the shelf. And you can't turn it down, because now your quietest whisper is as loud as a leaf blower. Day one, your little brother Sam thinks it's the coolest thing ever, so every night he falls asleep on your shoulder while you whisper him a bedtime story. Day thirty, at the beach, a rip current drags a swimmer out to sea, and he can't hear the lifeguard. You whisper, swim sideways, so he swims along the shore, and makes it back alive. But then something weird starts happening. Sam stops turning around when you call him. At dinner, he keeps asking, what? Because deep inside your ear are tiny hairs that turn sound into signals for your brain. Loud sound, night after night, flattens them like grass under a boot, and in humans, they never grow back. Day one hundred, you take Sam to his first big concert. Then you see smoke behind the stage. The music is so loud that nobody notices, and your whisper disappears in it. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. One full shout, and three thousand people run for the doors. Seven. Six. Sam is right beside you, covering his ears, but it won't be enough. Five. Four. Everyone else's ears will ring for a day. His will go silent forever. Sam's hearing, or three thousand strangers. Do you shout?
