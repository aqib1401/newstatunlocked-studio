SCRIPT:
What if you could hear a hundred times better? Not a little sharper. A hundred times. Day one, in a silent exam, you hear a pencil scratching three rooms away. Day two, your best friend Leo whispers from the back of the bus. Bet you can't hear this. You laugh at his joke before anyone else even hears it. Then, in the school kitchen, you hear a hiss nobody else can. A gas leak. Everyone, get out! Thirty kids walk out safe, because of you. Sounds amazing, right? Except quiet sounds aren't the only ones that got louder. Day five, a fire truck screams past, and your ears ring for hours. Sirens up close are loud enough to damage normal ears. Because deep inside each ear are thousands of tiny hair cells that turn sound into signals. Too much noise kills them, and once they die, they never grow back. So now you wear earplugs everywhere. Day nine, Leo's apartment block collapses. Rescuers stop the drills and beg for silence, but fire trucks keep screaming past. Somewhere under the rubble, Leo is tapping, so faintly that only you could find him, but only with your earplugs out. Next to those sirens, you could lose your hearing forever. No music. No voices. Not even your own. The tapping gets weaker. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. Your fingers touch the earplugs. Seven. Six. Leo is still knocking. Five. Four. Your best friend, or your hearing forever. Would you pull them out?
