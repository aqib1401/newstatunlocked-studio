SCRIPT:
What if you could rewind ten seconds? Any moment you want, just by blinking twice. Your phone slips off the table... blink blink, it's back in your hand. Lightning hits a tower right in front of you, but the flash is over in less than a second, way too fast to film. Blink blink. This time, your camera is ready. Then it's a penalty kick in a cup final. The ball flies in under half a second, so goalkeepers have to guess and dive early. You watch which way he dives, rewind, and kick the other way. Goal. The catch? Only you remember. So only you live those ten seconds twice. Rewind just a hundred times a day, and by the end of the year, you've lived four whole days that nobody else remembers. And that means something stranger. If someone else had this power, you would never know. Most people say they've felt deja vu, that eerie sense of living a moment twice. Scientists call it a memory glitch. So your friend who never, ever loses at rock paper scissors... watch how they blink.

FACTS USED:
1. A lightning flash lasts well under a second (a single stroke is microseconds; a full flash with several strokes is typically ~0.2-0.5 s): high
2. A well-struck penalty reaches the goal in under half a second, so keepers commit before seeing the ball's direction: high
3. 100 rewinds x 10 s = 1,000 s a day; x 365 = ~101 hours = ~4.2 days a year: high (math)
4. Surveys find roughly 60-70% of people report having experienced deja vu: high
5. Leading explanations of deja vu are memory-related glitches (familiarity without recall): medium-high
