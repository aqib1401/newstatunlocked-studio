What if you fell into a black hole? Straight in, no way back. The closest one we know of is about fifteen hundred light years away, so let's skip the trip... you're already floating above it. Attempt one: a small black hole, ten times heavier than the Sun but only sixty kilometers wide. You drift closer, and something starts tugging at your feet. Gravity down there is so much stronger than at your head that your body stretches, longer and thinner, like toothpaste squeezed from a tube. Scientists really call this spaghettification. And it gets you more than a thousand kilometers before you even reach the edge. So, attempt two: the giant black hole at the center of our galaxy, four million times heavier than the Sun. It's so huge that its pull feels almost the same on your head and your feet, so you fall in smoothly. You cross the edge, the event horizon... and you don't feel a thing. But your best friend Mia, watching from far away, sees something very different. To her, you slow down... and down... turning red, then dim, until you seem to freeze at the edge forever. Meanwhile, you're still falling. Inside, the center isn't a place you can swim away from anymore. It's in your future, like tomorrow, and nothing can stop tomorrow from coming. So how long do you get to enjoy the ride? From the moment you cross that edge... about one minute.
