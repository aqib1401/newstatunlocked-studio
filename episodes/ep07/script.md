What if you felt everyone else's pain? Not sympathy. The real thing. Your friend Leo stubs his toe, and you're the one hopping around the kitchen. At first, it's a superpower. Your mom swears she's fine, but you feel a stab low on her right side, so you drag her to the doctor. It's her appendix, and you caught it in time. But then crowds start to hurt. Every scraped knee in the room, all at once. Some people feel a touch just by watching someone else get touched. It's called mirror-touch synesthesia. Yours works for pain. Then your little brother Sam falls off his bike, and at the hospital, the doctor has to push his broken arm back into place. Sam squeezes your hand. The doctor counts. Three. Two. If you flinch, his arm moves. If you let go, he's alone. Feeling all of it, would you hold on?
