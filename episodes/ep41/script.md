SCRIPT:
What if you jumped straight through the Earth? You'd pop out on the other side... about forty minutes later. Every single time. Picture a tunnel nothing can melt, from the North Pole to the South Pole, with no air inside. It has to go pole to pole, because Earth spins, and any other tunnel would slam you into its wall. You step off the edge... and the ground is gone. For the first minute it feels like skydiving, except you never stop speeding up. Ten minutes in, the walls glow orange. The rock out there is hotter than lava. Then the walls turn to liquid metal, a sea of molten iron bigger than the Moon. You're falling about twenty times faster than sound. And right at the center... you weigh nothing. The whole planet pulls on you equally from every side, so you float, at full speed, straight through a solid iron ball as hot as the surface of the Sun. Now gravity is behind you, slowing you down. About forty minutes after you jumped, your head pops out in Antarctica, you stop for one perfect second... and fall back in. Again and again, forever. And here's the strangest part. Forty minutes is also how long a satellite skimming the ground takes to fly halfway around the world. Fall through the Earth, or fly around it... same clock.
