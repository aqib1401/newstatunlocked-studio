CAST: hero, Dr. Lee (your science teacher), your little brother Sam, two hiking-trip kids (extras)

SCRIPT:
What if you only had to eat once a year? Not once a day. Once a year. One giant meal, and your body stores the energy for twelve months. Day one, while everyone queues for lunch, you're alone on the basketball court, shooting free throws. Day ninety, a storm traps your school hiking trip on a mountain, and the food runs out. You're not hungry, so you hand every snack you packed to the other kids. Everyone eats. But by month eleven, your hands are always cold, and you can barely climb the stairs. Your tank is running empty. Back at school, Dr. Lee, your science teacher, does the math. A year of energy is over seven hundred thousand calories. That's about three hundred large pizzas, in one day. But a normal stomach holds about a litre, and stretches to about four, like a balloon. So meal day isn't a feast. It's a race against midnight, because whatever you don't eat, your body goes without for a whole year. Day three hundred sixty-five, you eat from sunrise. By eleven at night, you're only halfway, when your phone rings. It's your little brother Sam. Please come get me. He's alone at the train station, an hour away, Mom's not answering, and his battery is about to die. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. If you go, you'll never finish before midnight. Seven. Six. Sam starts to cry. Five. Four. Your little brother, or a year on half a tank. Do you stop eating?
