SCRIPT:
What if you could never feel cold? Not even a little. In January, everyone at the frozen lake is shivering, and you're sitting on the ice in shorts, eating a double scoop of ice cream. Day one, you win every snowball fight, because your bare hands can pack snow for an hour. Day ten, your school bus breaks down on a frozen mountain road, so you jog to the nearest farm in a T-shirt and bring back a tractor. Twenty kids cheer. But that night, something weird starts happening. Your best friend Mia grabs your hand, and screams. Your fingertips are white and hard as candle wax, and you didn't feel a thing. Because cold isn't just a feeling. It's an alarm. Nerves in your skin warn your brain, so you shiver and grab a coat. Without that alarm, your skin can freeze and die with zero pain. Day thirty, back at the frozen lake, Mia's little dog Biscuit chases a duck onto the thin ice. Crack. He drops through, and his paws scratch at the edge. Nobody moves, except you. Your friend Leo grabs your arm. Don't. In water this cold, your arms can stop working within minutes, and you won't feel it coming. Ten seconds. Nine. Eight. Biscuit's head slips under, then pops back up. Seven. Six. Mia is screaming his name. Five. Four. If you go in, nothing will tell you when to get out. Mia's dog, or your own life. Do you jump in?
