What if you were a thousand times stronger? Strong enough to hold a bus over your head with one hand. And the thing that finally beats you isn't a wall, or a villain... it's your dad. Second one. You wake up, stretch, and your fist goes straight through the wall. You sit up, and the bed frame snaps like a breadstick. Minute five. You're late, and the bus is pulling away, so you jump. The sidewalk cracks like ice under your shoes, because concrete can't take a push that strong. The air drags on you, but you still shoot past two hundred meters, higher than a sixty story skyscraper. Then you fall, at about two hundred kilometers an hour, the top speed of a person falling belly down. Your bones got a thousand times tougher too, so you're fine. The park you land in... now has a crater. Day ten. You throw a baseball, and it leaves your hand so fast it breaks the sound barrier, and the whole stadium hears a boom. Day thirty. Your best friend Mia holds up her hand for a high five, and you spend a whole week practicing on eggs before you dare. Year one. You can catch a falling piano, and you can hold a butterfly. So at the family picnic, you finally say yes to a tug of war against your dad. You pull with the strength of a crane... and you slide straight across the grass toward him. Because tug of war isn't won with your arms. It's won by pushing on the ground, and Dad weighs more. So you cheat. You walk over, pick up your dad, and carry him across the line.
