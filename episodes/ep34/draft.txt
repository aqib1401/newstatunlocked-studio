What if you were a thousand times stronger? Strong enough to hold a bus over your head with one hand. And the thing that finally beats you isn't a wall, or a villain... it's your dad. Second one. You wake up, stretch, and your fist goes straight through the wall. You sit up, and the bed frame snaps like a breadstick. Minute five. You squeeze the toothpaste, and it blasts across the whole mirror. Because a normal hand squeezes with about forty kilograms of force... and yours now squeezes with forty tonnes, about as heavy as a fully loaded truck. So the hardest skill in your life is now holding things. Minute twenty. You're late, and the bus is pulling away, so you jump. Your legs push a thousand times harder, and the sidewalk cracks like ice under your shoes, because concrete can't take a push that strong. You shoot up over two hundred meters, higher than a sixty story skyscraper. Then you fall. The air slows you down to about two hundred kilometers an hour, the top speed of any falling human. You're fine. The park you land in... now has a crater. Hour two. A car has broken down in the middle of the road, so you push it. And here's the part nobody expects. Your feet skid, and you slide backwards. Pushing needs grip, and grip comes from your weight... and you weigh exactly the same as yesterday. So you lift it instead. You grab the bumper and it tears off like paper, because a car's skin is steel about one millimeter thin. You slide your hands under the frame and carry it to the curb like a pizza box. Day one. Ten million people watch the video. Day ten. Builders hire you to carry steel beams up a tower, and firefighters give you your own helmet. Day thirty. Your best friend Mia holds up her hand for a high five, and you spend a whole week practicing on eggs before you dare. Year one. You can catch a falling piano, and you can hold a butterfly. So at the family picnic, you finally say yes to a tug of war against your dad. You pull with the strength of a crane... and you slide straight across the grass toward him. Because tug of war isn't won with your arms. It's won by pushing on the ground, and Dad weighs more. So you do the only fair thing. You walk over, pick up your dad, and carry him across the line.
